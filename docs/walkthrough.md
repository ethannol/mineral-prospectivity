# Five-minute walkthrough

_Rendered from `walkthrough.tmpl.md`; every number is a ledger value._

**0:00 to 0:30. The question.** Prospectivity models rank ground by how much it looks like known deposits. The usual validation is random k-fold. Deposits cluster, stream-sediment chemistry is spatially smooth, so random folds put a district's cells on both sides of the split. I wanted to measure how much of a model's reported skill is that leakage, on public data, with the protocol fixed before I fitted anything.

**0:30 to 1:30. The data.** USGS NURE stream sediments and USGS MRDS deposit records, Nevada. Nevada because its two DOE labs share a usable element suite across all 18 quadrangles and it has 2850 gold producer sites; Arizona had four labs and half its samples were soils. The honest catch: once I grouped samples by analytical method family, only ten elements (Al, Ce, Fe, La, Mn, Na, Sc, Th, Ti, V) were measured consistently, and none is a gold pathfinder. Censoring is coded as negative numbers in NURE; 3.6 percent of suite values are censored, worst Ti at 21 percent in one family, substituted at half the limit and reported.

**1:30 to 2:30. Units, labels, pre-registration.** The unit is a 5 km cell with the mean centred log-ratio of its samples, which removes within-site pseudo-replication: 6378 cells, 671 dropped as ambiguous (gold prospects or occurrences but no producer), 5707 in the population, 553 positive. Negatives are unlabelled, not barren; I say so wherever precision is quoted. The deposit join is a per-cell count merged one-to-one and checked. I froze the question, the unit, the labels, the suite rule, both model grids, the CV protocol and the decision rule in `docs/prereg.md` with cairn before fitting anything, including how the reference block size would be chosen: the smallest block at or above the distance where Moran's I of the label drops below 0.05. That turned out to be 70 km, so the reference is 100 km.

**2:30 to 3:45. The headline.** Random 5-fold CV gives the boosted model an AUC of 0.638, range 0.627 to 0.648 across 5 repetitions. Blocks at 100 km, with a new offset and allocation each repetition, give 0.571, range 0.554 to 0.590. The ranges do not overlap, the drop is 0.068, and only 51 percent of the skill above chance survives. The capture curve says the same thing in a geologist's units: the top ten percent of cells hold 21% of held-out producers under random CV and 18% under block CV. By the rule I wrote down in advance, that is "substantial" leakage and "no demonstrated skill beyond chance once spatial structure is respected". The logistic regression tells the same story at 0.610 to 0.559.

**3:45 to 4:30. The second trap, and what the model learned.** Hold out an entire analytical family and the pooled AUC is 0.488; add the family as a one-hot covariate and random-CV AUC rises by 0.031. The model can learn the lab. The elements with signal, V, Sc, Fe enriched and La, Ce, Th depleted in producer cells, are a mafic-versus-felsic lithology contrast, consistent with volcanic-hosted districts, not a hydrothermal signature. Lithology is spatially smooth, which is exactly why random CV overstates it.

**4:30 to 5:00. What it is for.** Not an exploration map. It is a measured demonstration that spatial block CV is mandatory for this kind of model, a baseline for a pathfinder-bearing dataset, and a reproducible pipeline: committed extract with checksums, frozen pre-registration, every number recorded to a ledger by the script that computed it, a clean re-execution that reproduces them, and 19 tests including a synthetic leakage demonstration.


The frozen skill label is an operational decision rule, not a statistical test that AUC equals 0.5. The reference model does not meet the pre-registered useful-skill threshold. Family indicators are learnable proxies for both method and region; this dataset cannot isolate a causal laboratory effect. See [the supplementary distance-buffer sensitivity](docs/buffered-validation.md) for a stricter exploratory separation check.
