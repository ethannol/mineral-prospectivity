# Five-minute walkthrough

_Rendered from `walkthrough.tmpl.md`; every number is a ledger value._

**0:00 to 0:30. The question.** Prospectivity models rank ground by how much it looks like known deposits. The usual validation is random k-fold. Deposits cluster, stream-sediment chemistry is spatially smooth, so random folds put a district's cells on both sides of the split. I wanted to measure how much of a model's reported skill is that leakage, on public data, with the protocol fixed before I fitted anything.

**0:30 to 1:30. The data.** USGS NURE stream sediments and USGS MRDS deposit records, Nevada. Nevada because its two DOE labs share a usable element suite across all {{extract.nure_quads}} quadrangles and it has {{labels.n_positive_sites}} gold producer sites; Arizona had four labs and half its samples were soils. The honest catch: once I grouped samples by analytical method family, only ten elements ({{prep.suite_elements}}) were measured consistently, and none is a gold pathfinder. Censoring is coded as negative numbers in NURE; {{prep.censored_pct_overall:.1f}} percent of suite values are censored, worst {{prep.censored_pct_max_element}} at {{prep.censored_pct_max:.0f}} percent in one family, substituted at half the limit and reported.

**1:30 to 2:30. Units, labels, pre-registration.** The unit is a {{features.cell_km:g}} km cell with the mean centred log-ratio of its samples, which removes within-site pseudo-replication: {{features.n_cells}} cells, {{labels.n_cells_ambiguous}} dropped as ambiguous (gold prospects or occurrences but no producer), {{labels.n_cells}} in the population, {{labels.n_positive_cells}} positive. Negatives are unlabelled, not barren; I say so wherever precision is quoted. The deposit join is a per-cell count merged one-to-one and checked. I froze the question, the unit, the labels, the suite rule, both model grids, the CV protocol and the decision rule in `docs/prereg.md` with cairn before fitting anything, including how the reference block size would be chosen: the smallest block at or above the distance where Moran's I of the label drops below {{moran.threshold:g}}. That turned out to be {{moran.range_km:g}} km, so the reference is {{headline.reference_block_km:g}} km.

**2:30 to 3:45. The headline.** Random {{cv.k_outer}}-fold CV gives the boosted model an AUC of {{cv.hgb.random.auc.mean:.3f}}, range {{cv.hgb.random.auc.min:.3f}} to {{cv.hgb.random.auc.max:.3f}} across {{cv.n_repetitions}} repetitions. Blocks at {{headline.reference_block_km:g}} km, with a new offset and allocation each repetition, give {{cv.hgb.ref.auc.mean:.3f}}, range {{cv.hgb.ref.auc.min:.3f}} to {{cv.hgb.ref.auc.max:.3f}}. The ranges do not overlap, the drop is {{headline.hgb.auc_inflation:.3f}}, and only {{headline.hgb.pct_skill_surviving:.0f}} percent of the skill above chance survives. The capture curve says the same thing in a geologist's units: the top ten percent of cells hold {{cv.hgb.random.capture_10.mean:.0%}} of held-out producers under random CV and {{cv.hgb.ref.capture_10.mean:.0%}} under block CV. By the rule I wrote down in advance, that is "{{headline.hgb.leakage_label}}" leakage and "{{headline.hgb.skill_label}}". The logistic regression tells the same story at {{cv.lr.random.auc.mean:.3f}} to {{cv.lr.ref.auc.mean:.3f}}.

**3:45 to 4:30. The second trap, and what the model learned.** Hold out an entire analytical family and the pooled AUC is {{batch.hgb.lofo.auc:.3f}}; add the family as a one-hot covariate and random-CV AUC rises by {{explore.labcov.hgb.random_gain:.3f}}. The model can learn the lab. The elements with signal, {{explore.contrast_most_enriched}} enriched and {{explore.contrast_most_depleted}} depleted in producer cells, are a mafic-versus-felsic lithology contrast, consistent with volcanic-hosted districts, not a hydrothermal signature. Lithology is spatially smooth, which is exactly why random CV overstates it.

**4:30 to 5:00. What it is for.** Not an exploration map. It is a measured demonstration that spatial block CV is mandatory for this kind of model, a baseline for a pathfinder-bearing dataset, and a reproducible pipeline: committed extract with checksums, frozen pre-registration, every number recorded to a ledger by the script that computed it, a clean re-execution that reproduces them, and {{tests.n_passed}} tests including a synthetic leakage demonstration.
